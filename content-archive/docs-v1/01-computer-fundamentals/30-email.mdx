# Email

## Overview

**Email (Electronic Mail)** is a way of sending digital messages over the internet from one person to another. Instead of writing a letter and mailing it physically, you type a message on a device, and it travels instantly through the internet to arrive in the recipient's inbox — often within seconds, no matter how far apart you are.

Think of email like a digital version of the postal system. Every person has a unique email address (like a house address), which tells the system exactly where to deliver the message. When you hit "Send," your message doesn't go directly to the recipient's device — it first travels to a **mail server**, gets sorted, and is then delivered to the recipient's mail server, where it waits for them to check their inbox.

Email is one of the oldest and still most widely used forms of internet communication, used for everything from personal messages to business communication, account verifications, and newsletters.

## Key Concepts

- **Email Address** — A unique identifier in the format `username@domain.com`, telling the system who the message is for and which mail service handles it.
- **Mail Server** — A specialized server responsible for sending, receiving, and storing email messages, acting as the digital "post office."
- **Inbox** — Where your received emails are stored and displayed, ready for you to read.
- **Attachments** — Files (documents, photos, etc.) that can be included alongside an email message.
- **SMTP, IMAP, POP3** — Protocols (shared rules) that control how email is sent (SMTP) and retrieved (IMAP/POP3) between devices and mail servers.
- **Spam/Junk Filter** — A system that automatically detects and separates unwanted or suspicious emails, keeping your main inbox cleaner and safer.
