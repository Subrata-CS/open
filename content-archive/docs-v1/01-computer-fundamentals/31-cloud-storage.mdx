# Cloud Storage

## Overview

**Cloud Storage** lets you save files on the internet instead of only on your own device's hard drive or SSD. Services like Google Drive, Dropbox, and iCloud store your data on powerful remote servers (called "the cloud"), which you can access from any device with an internet connection — your laptop, phone, or a friend's computer.

Think of cloud storage like renting a storage unit in another city, except this "unit" is accessible instantly from anywhere, at any time, through the internet. Instead of only being able to open your files on the one computer where you saved them, cloud storage lets you upload a file from your laptop and open that same file minutes later on your phone, without needing to physically transfer it yourself.

Behind the scenes, "the cloud" isn't some magical floating place — it's simply someone else's data center, full of thousands of powerful servers, constantly running and maintained by companies so your files stay safe and available.

## Key Concepts

- **Upload** — Sending a copy of your file from your device to the cloud storage server, over the internet.
- **Download** — Retrieving a file from the cloud storage server back onto your device.
- **Sync** — Automatically keeping files updated and identical across all your devices, so a change made on one device appears everywhere else too.
- **Data Center** — A large facility full of servers where cloud companies physically store and manage your data.
- **Backup** — Cloud storage is often used to keep a safe copy of important files, protecting them even if your physical device is lost, stolen, or damaged.
- **Sharing/Collaboration** — Cloud storage often allows you to share files or folders with others, letting multiple people view or edit the same document.
