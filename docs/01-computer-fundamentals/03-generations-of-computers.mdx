# Generations of Computers

<Lede>
The development of computers is divided into **five generations** based on the major electronic technology used. Every new generation made computers smaller, faster, cheaper and more reliable.
</Lede>

## Overview

A **generation of computers** refers to a stage in the evolution of computers distinguished by the technology used in their electronic circuits.

<Callout kind="def" title="Definition">
A computer generation is the classification of computers according to the main hardware technology used during a particular period.
</Callout>

## First Generation (1940–1956)

<Fig
  label="Fig 2"
  title="First generation computers"
  caption="Vacuum tubes were large, consumed huge amounts of electricity and generated excessive heat."
>
  <AsyncImageGroup query={["ENIAC computer room","Vacuum tube electronic component","UNIVAC I historical photograph"]} layout=carousel/>
</Fig>

The **first generation** used **vacuum tubes** as its primary electronic component. These computers occupied entire rooms and required continuous cooling.

#### Key characteristics

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Technology | Vacuum Tube |
| Period | 1940–1956 |
| Size | Very large |
| Speed | Milliseconds |
| Memory | Magnetic drum |
| Language | Machine language |

#### Advantages

- First electronic computer
- Faster than electromechanical machines
- Automatic calculations

#### Limitations

- Consumed enormous electricity
- Produced excessive heat
- Very expensive
- Required frequent maintenance

**Examples:** ENIAC, EDVAC, UNIVAC I

---

## Second Generation (1956–1963)

<Fig
  label="Fig 3"
  title="Second generation computers"
  caption="The invention of the transistor made computers smaller, cooler and much more reliable."
>
  <AsyncImageGroup query={["Transistor electronic component","IBM 1401 computer","Second generation computer historical"]} layout=carousel/>
</Fig>

The **second generation** replaced vacuum tubes with **transistors**.

<Callout kind="note" title="Why was the transistor important?">
A transistor performed the same job as a vacuum tube while being much smaller, consuming less power and producing very little heat.
</Callout>

#### Features

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Technology | Transistor |
| Size | Smaller |
| Speed | Microseconds |
| Reliability | Higher |
| Languages | FORTRAN, COBOL |

**Examples:** IBM 1401, IBM 7094

---

## Third Generation (1964–1971)

<Fig
  label="Fig 4"
  title="Third generation computers"
  caption="Integrated Circuits (ICs) combined thousands of electronic components onto a single silicon chip."
>
  <AsyncImageGroup query={["Integrated circuit chip close up","IBM System 360 computer","IC semiconductor illustration"]} layout=carousel/>
</Fig>

The **third generation** introduced **Integrated Circuits (ICs)**, replacing individual transistors.

#### Major improvements

- Keyboard and monitor became common
- Operating systems developed
- Multiprogramming introduced
- Higher storage capacity
- Smaller and faster machines

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Technology | IC |
| Speed | Nanoseconds |
| Memory | Semiconductor memory |
| Example | IBM System/360 |

---

## Fourth Generation (1971–Present)

<Fig
  label="Fig 5"
  title="Fourth generation computers"
  caption="A complete CPU could now fit onto a single microprocessor chip, leading to personal computers."
>
  <AsyncImageGroup query={["Intel 4004 microprocessor","Desktop personal computer","Modern laptop motherboard"]} layout=carousel/>
</Fig>

The **fourth generation** began with the invention of the **microprocessor**.

#### What changed?

- CPU placed on a single chip
- Personal computers introduced
- GUI operating systems developed
- Networking became common
- Very high processing speed

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| Technology | Microprocessor |
| Speed | Picoseconds |
| Devices | Desktop, Laptop, Tablet |
| Example | IBM PC, Apple Macintosh |

## Fifth Generation (Present & Future)

<Fig
  label="Fig 6"
  title="Fifth generation computers"

  <AsyncImageGroup query={["Artificial intelligence robot digital interface","AI neural network visualization","Modern humanoid AI concept"]} layout=carousel/>

The **fifth generation** focuses on **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** rather than only increasing hardware speed.

#### AI technologies

- Machine Learning
- Deep Learning
- Natural Language Processing
- Computer Vision
- Robotics
- Expert Systems

<Callout kind="tip" title="Exam point">
The **fifth generation** is identified by **Artificial Intelligence**, not simply by faster processors.
</Callout>

## Memory Trick

<Callout kind="tip" title="Remember the order">
**V → T → IC → MP → AI**

- **V** = Vacuum Tube
- **T** = Transistor
- **IC** = Integrated Circuit
- **MP** = Microprocessor
- **AI** = Artificial Intelligence
</Callout>

---

## Example

Suppose an exam asks:

**Why is the fourth generation called the Microprocessor Generation?**

**Answer:**

> The fourth generation is called the Microprocessor Generation because the complete CPU was integrated onto a single silicon chip, making computers smaller, faster, cheaper and suitable for personal use.
