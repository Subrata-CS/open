# Mouse

## Overview

The **Mouse** is an **input device** that lets you point, click, and interact with things on the screen. Instead of typing characters like a keyboard, the mouse tells the computer *where* on the screen you want to interact, and *what action* you want to take there — moving, clicking, dragging, or scrolling.

Think of the mouse as your hand reaching into the screen. When you move the mouse on your desk, a matching cursor moves on the screen. When you click, you're telling the computer "do something right here" — like opening a file, pressing a button, or selecting text.

Almost every visual interaction with a computer — outside of typing — relies on the mouse (or a similar pointing device like a trackpad or trackball).

## Key Concepts

- **Cursor/Pointer** — The small arrow (or icon) on screen that moves in sync with the mouse, showing exactly where your next click will happen.
- **Click (Left/Right)** — Left-click usually selects or activates something; right-click usually opens a menu of extra options.
- **Double-Click** — Clicking twice quickly, commonly used to open files or programs.
- **Drag and Drop** — Holding the click button while moving the mouse, used to move files, resize windows, or select multiple items.
- **Scroll Wheel** — The wheel between the two buttons, used to scroll up and down through pages or documents.
- **DPI (Dots Per Inch)** — Measures mouse sensitivity — higher DPI means the cursor moves further on screen for the same physical mouse movement.
- **Types of Mice** — Includes optical mice (use a light sensor), wireless mice (connect via Bluetooth/USB receiver), and trackpads (built into laptops, using finger touch instead of physical movement).
