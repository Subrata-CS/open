# Printer

## Overview

A **Printer** is an **output device** that takes digital information from a computer — text, images, documents — and turns it into a physical, paper copy. Unlike a monitor, which shows information temporarily on screen, a printer creates something permanent that you can hold, file, or hand to someone.

Think of a printer as a translator between the digital world and the physical world. The computer stores your document as data (a pattern of pixels and text), and the printer's job is to recreate that exact pattern using ink or toner on paper, dot by dot or line by line.

Printers are one of the oldest and still most common output devices, used everywhere from homes and offices to schools and hospitals.

## Key Concepts

- **Inkjet Printer** — Sprays tiny droplets of liquid ink onto paper to form text and images. Common for home use, good for photos, but can be slower and more expensive per page.
- **Laser Printer** — Uses a laser and toner powder (fused onto paper with heat) to print. Faster and cheaper per page for large volumes, commonly used in offices.
- **Print Driver** — Software installed on the computer that translates your document into a format the specific printer model understands.
- **Print Queue** — A waiting list managed by the operating system when multiple print jobs are sent one after another; each job prints in order.
- **DPI (Dots Per Inch)** — Measures print quality — higher DPI means more detail and sharper printed text/images.
- **3D Printer** — A newer type of printer that builds physical objects layer by layer using materials like plastic, instead of printing on flat paper.
