# Internet Basics

## Overview

The **Internet** is a massive network of computers all over the world, connected together so they can share information with each other. When you visit a website, send a message, or watch a video online, your computer is talking to another computer (a **server**) somewhere else in the world, exchanging data back and forth through this network.

Think of the internet like a giant postal system. When you write a letter (a request, like typing a website address), it doesn't go directly to its destination in one jump — it passes through many post offices and sorting centers (routers) along the way, each one reading the address and forwarding it closer to its destination. The reply (the website's data) travels back to you the same way.

No single company or computer "owns" the internet — it's a cooperative network of millions of interconnected devices, all following shared rules (called **protocols**) so they can understand each other.

## Key Concepts

- **Server** — A powerful computer that stores websites, files, or data, and responds to requests from other computers (like yours).
- **Client** — Your device (phone, laptop) that requests information from a server, such as asking to load a webpage.
- **IP Address** — A unique numerical address assigned to every device on a network, similar to a postal address, so data knows where to go.
- **Router** — A device that directs data between networks, forwarding information toward its correct destination, step by step.
- **ISP (Internet Service Provider)** — The company (like your local broadband or mobile provider) that connects your home or device to the wider internet.
- **Protocol** — A shared set of rules that computers follow to communicate correctly, such as **HTTP/HTTPS** (used for websites) or **TCP/IP** (the underlying rules for sending data reliably across the internet).
